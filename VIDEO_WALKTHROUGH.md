# Six minute walkthrough notes

Use these as checkpoints, not a script to read. Keep the browser and your camera visible. Use a fictional prospect and do not show real contact or prospect data.

| Time | Show | Explain in your own words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:35 | Callside home | Who the caller is, what the screen helps with, and the choice not to record audio. |
| 0:35–1:00 | Start a sample call | Enter “Jordan Lee” and “Northstar Studio.” Explain why the setup only asks for a name and optional company. |
| 1:00–2:00 | Open and permission stages | Point out stage purpose, a flexible prompt, likely prospect responses, and the one small action. Keep the app alongside a pretend call. |

| 4:10–5:00 | Objections and next step | Demonstrate acknowledging an objection, respecting a no, and agreeing on a concrete next action when there is interest. |
| 5:00–5:35 | End call and history | Save the outcome and show the reached stage and notes. Mention that a stage saves as the caller moves on. |
| 5:35–6:00 | One product decision | Explain the main design choice you made, what evidence from the supplied transcripts/guide informed it, and what you would improve next. |

## Decision to discuss

The interface keeps the stage purpose and prospect-language examples visible beside the caller's notes and outcome control. That supports a quick glance during a live conversation. The prompts are phrased as ways in, not mandatory lines, because a caller needs room to listen and adapt. Once the assignment's 100 fictionalized transcripts are reviewed, replace the starter response examples with evidence from those calls and describe one concrete pattern you found.

## Before recording

- Set the browser zoom and window size so the active stage, webcam, and call controls are easy to see.
- Use a fictional prospect. The stage guide currently reflects the screenshots only; the linked stage guide, reference screen, and transcripts have not been provided in this workspace.
- Keep the walkthrough conversational. Pause to show the interface and explain why you chose a behavior.
- Upload the finished camera-on recording to Google Drive and set “Anyone with the link can view,” as requested by the assignment. Add the final link to the assignment form and required WhatsApp message yourself.
